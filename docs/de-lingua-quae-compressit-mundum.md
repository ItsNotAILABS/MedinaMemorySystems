The IPv4 address is four bytes (32 bits). Those four bytes encode: the network identity, the host identity, the routing class, the broadcast boundary. Before IPv4, routing tables were described in prose. After IPv4, a packet's entire delivery contract fit in four bytes.

TCP/IP did not abbreviate routing. It **compressed** it. The four bytes are not shorthand — they are the routing doctrine itself, encoded in a form machines can process in microseconds.

The IETF understood this: they called the design *elegant*, not *short*. Elegance in information systems means: **maximum doctrine per bit**.

*Pacta sunt servanda.* ("Agreements must be kept.")

Three words. They encode: the binding force of private contracts, the primacy of consent over status, the enforceability of promises across jurisdictions, the foundation of commercial civilization. Every contract attorney in every common-law and civil-law jurisdiction operates within the weight of these three words.

Roman jurists did not summarize law. They **compressed** it. The maxim is not a shortcut to understanding *pacta sunt servanda* — it **is** the understanding, collapsed to its irreducible form.

The Corpus Juris Civilis understood this: the most important legal rules are the ones short enough to be memorized and applied without looking them up.

## VI. CONCLUSIO — Why Mature Systems Converge on Compressed Sovereign Language

The convergence is not accidental. It follows from three constraints that all mature information systems face:

### Constraint 1: Cognitive Bandwidth

Humans can hold approximately 7 ± 2 chunks in working memory (Miller, 1956). As a system's doctrine grows beyond 7 concepts, practitioners must either:

- (a) Compress the doctrine into fewer, denser symbols, or
- (b) Lose track of parts of the doctrine.

Option (b) is system failure. Therefore mature systems converge on option (a) — not by choice, but by necessity.

### Constraint 2: Interface Surface

Every module boundary, function signature, and log message is a compression decision. The name chosen for a concept determines how many bits of context a reader must hold to understand the system. A name that encodes its own doctrine (like *φ*, like *SL-0*) minimizes the external context required.

TCP/IP chose 32-bit addresses rather than human-readable names precisely because 32 bits is the right compression for routing context — long enough to be unique, short enough to be fast.

### Constraint 3: Temporal Durability

Prose descriptions drift. As systems evolve, prose comments become stale, outdated, misleading. A symbol — especially one with documented doctrine — drifts more slowly because its meaning is defined by the lexicon, not by the surrounding text.

Latin was chosen as the naming substrate for MEDINA precisely because:
- It has no native speakers → no semantic drift
- Inflectional morphology encodes grammatical role into the word itself
- Cross-domain authority (medicine, law, science have already proved Latin names last)
- Self-documenting for anyone who reads the root
